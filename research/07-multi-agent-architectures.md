# 07 — Multi-agent architectures: what the evidence supports
> Slice: MultiAgentResearch · Researched 2026-10-08 · Scope: Definitions, empirical benefits and limits, coding-specific evidence, model-role selection, review, context handoffs, and cost-aware architectural principles.

## Narration-ready takeaways

- “Mixture-of-Agents” originally names a specific layered proposal-and-synthesis method, not every system with subagents. Wang and colleagues’ paper was published at ICLR 2025; its agents exchange generated answers through prompts rather than requiring autonomous tool-use loops. [1]
- More inference can improve an answer without improving the underlying model: the original MoA study reported 65.1% on AlpacaEval 2.0 against GPT-4 Omni’s 57.5%. Those are historical, judge-based instruction-following results—not repository-repair success rates. [1]
- Mixing model brands is not automatically better. Self-MoA found that repeatedly sampling the strongest proposer could outperform mixing six different proposers, while X-MAS found benefits from selecting models for specific roles and domains. [2][6]
- “More Agents Is All You Need” studies sampling and voting, not a swarm of programmers editing a shared repository. Its results support an ensemble baseline; they do not establish that adding autonomous collaborators always helps. [3]
- Debate can improve reasoning, but the baseline matters. An ICML study found gains from debate, while an ACL study found that a strongly prompted single model could nearly match the best discussion systems. [4][5]
- Anthropic reported a 90.2% improvement for an Opus 4 lead with Sonnet 4 research workers over a single Opus 4 on its internal research evaluation. It also reported that multi-agent systems used about 15 times the tokens of chat—not 15 times the tokens of a single agent. [9]
- A useful coding division of labor can be a strong planner, cheaper repository scouts, a capable editor, and an evidence-seeking reviewer. HyperAgent provides an early measured example, but neither its model choices nor its agent count should be treated as a universal recipe. [19]
- Cognition’s position changed: its June 2025 warning about multi-agents was followed in April 2026 by support for clean-context review and advisory agents around a single writer. Quoting only “Don’t Build Multi-Agents” would misrepresent its current position. [10][11]
- An LLM’s approval is not proof that code works. Peer-reviewed studies identify self-preference and superficial code-evaluation biases, while Agent-as-a-Judge shows the value of letting evaluators inspect actual artifacts and requirements. [12][17][18]
- The most defensible recommendation is conditional: parallelize separable work, keep ownership and handoffs explicit, verify the integrated result, and measure whether the extra coordination pays for itself. That is a synthesis of the evidence, not a claim that one architecture has won. [7][8][9][11][19]

## Findings

### 1. Definitions: separate mechanisms before comparing results

| Term | Operational meaning | What it does not imply |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mixture-of-Agents (MoA), Wang et al.** | Multiple proposers generate answers; later layers read previous-layer answers and synthesize improved answers; a final aggregator produces the output. Models can be reused within and across layers. The evaluated method works through prompts, without fine-tuning. [1] | Not neural mixture-of-experts, not necessarily different model families, and not necessarily tool-using agents. The paper’s dense proposal network is not the same as choosing a single cheap model for a query. [1] |
| **Multi-agent system (broad sense)** | Multiple LLM-backed decision processes communicate through messages, shared memory, or orchestration; architectures include independent branches, centralized orchestrators, decentralized peers, and hybrids. Definitions vary: the scaling study distinguishes sustained environmental interaction from static answer generation. [8] | Multiple calls in a single decision loop do not automatically create independent agents; neither do parallel tool calls alone. [8][9] |
| **Orchestrator–worker** | A lead dynamically decomposes the task, delegates bounded work, and integrates results. Anthropic’s Research implementation gives workers separate contexts. [9] | Not an obligation to delegate every task; its authors explicitly warn about dependency-heavy work and unnecessary spawning. [9] |
| **Sampling/voting ensemble** | Generate multiple candidate answers, then select by majority or similarity. Agent Forest uses frequency for closed-ended answers and cumulative BLEU similarity for open-ended generation, including code. [3] | Not collaborative debate, and BLEU agreement is not executable correctness. [3] |
| **Debate** | Agents independently answer, read peers’ answers, and revise over rounds; Du et al. use a majority answer if consensus is not reached. [4] | Agreement does not guarantee truth; the paper explicitly illustrates convergence to a fact that need not be correct. [4] |
| **Role pipeline** | Agents enact different stages or responsibilities. MetaGPT encodes standard operating procedures and intermediate checks; ChatDev uses a chat chain spanning design, coding, and testing. [13][14] | A human-sounding job title alone is not evidence that the split adds value; MAST documents role, communication, and verification failures in such systems. [7] |
| **Context-isolated subagent** | A child explores or reviews in its own context and returns selected findings/artifacts. Its benefit can be keeping exploration out of the main history, even without simultaneous execution. [9][10][11] | Context isolation is distinct from model diversity; two instances of the same model can have different evidence and trajectories. [9][11] |
| **Model routing** | RouteLLM learns a preference-based choice between a stronger and weaker model for a query. [15] | Routing need not involve multiple answers, discussion, or a worker team. [15] |
| **Model cascade** | FrugalGPT calls models in an optimized order and stops when a learned answer-quality scorer crosses a threshold. [16] | A cascade can call several models, but it is not a debate or a layered synthesis network. [16] |
| **Advisor–executor** | A lower-cost executor retains the task and tool loop, consulting a stronger model for guidance. Anthropic’s advisor does not call tools or produce the user-facing answer. [21] | This inverts the usual strong-orchestrator/cheap-worker arrangement and need not decompose the task at all. [21] |

**[INFERENCE] Recommended vocabulary for the film:** call omp’s general capability “multi-agent orchestration” or “model-specialized subagents.” Reserve capitalized “Mixture-of-Agents” for the proposal–aggregation method, unless deliberately explaining a broader informal usage. Whether omp implements a particular topology is a separate product/source question, not demonstrated by the MoA paper. [1][8]

### 3. What is actually supported by the positive results?

**MoA is a result about synthesis, not just selection.** Its paper distinguishes proposer capability from aggregator capability and reports that aggregation beats asking a ranker to select a candidate. Its final version adds Arena-Hard results beyond the initial arXiv treatment. Nevertheless, the evaluated outputs are primarily judged responses; transfer to tool-driven repository work requires separate evidence. [1]

**Self-MoA does not say diversity is useless.** It finds positive associations with both quality and diversity, but a quality–diversity trade-off makes mixing weaker proposers counterproductive in many tested configurations. It also describes circumstances where mixed models help. Importantly, “Self” in its headline AlpacaEval experiment refers to the proposer samples: the aggregator is a different model. [2]

**X-MAS asks a different question.** It changes the model assigned to each functional role—QA, revision, aggregation, planning, evaluation—rather than indiscriminately mixing all candidates. Its coding experiments include MBPP, not SWE-bench repository repair. Its headline “47%” should not be quoted without a specific row/baseline; the tabled values and settings are safer narration material. **[INFERENCE]** X-MAS and Self-MoA are compatible with the principle “preserve competence while adding useful diversity,” rather than contradictory universal laws. [2][6]

**Sampling and voting are necessary baselines.** Agent Forest compares multiple samples against one query and states that token consumption grows proportionally with ensemble size. It also reports that adding debate to some Llama configurations damaged code coherence and failed on HumanEval. Even a paper titled “More Agents Is All You Need” therefore contains important limits on interaction. [3]

**Context isolation has a plausible mechanism and some direct coding evidence.** Anthropic describes workers as compression filters: each searches its own space and returns salient information to the lead. HyperAgent’s role ablation transfers a removed worker’s tools to the planner rather than simply deleting functionality; success decreases and cost increases. For Lite-1 on SWE-bench Tiny, removing Navigator changes **24.00% / $0.48** to **9.00% / $1.32**. This supports the particular partition, not a proof that every extra context window is beneficial. [9][19]

### 4. Coding architectures: planning, scouting, execution, and the single-writer boundary

**Early measured role specialization.** HyperAgent uses a centralized planner, repository navigator, editor, and executor, with Context/Request handoffs and a lightweight summarizer. The Lite configuration reduces model cost in navigation and execution while retaining stronger planning/editing. Its authors also report early completion hallucinations, timeouts, and lost execution details in summaries; reducing the planner’s context is useful only if important evidence survives. [19]

**Do not treat cross-paper score tables as controlled experiments.** HyperAgent’s own table contains a competitive, cheaper Agentless baseline; Agentless’s accepted abstract reports different figures from its earlier arXiv version. Different models, patch-selection methods, prompts, evaluation dates, and benchmark variants prevent a causal claim that “four agents beat one” from those leaderboard-style comparisons. [19][20]

**Current advisor evidence is stronger than an unmeasured recipe, but still confounded.** Anthropic’s 2026 advisor release evaluates an executor plus stronger advisor on both SWE-bench Multilingual and Terminal-Bench 2.0. For Terminal-Bench it reports five attempts across 89 tasks, isolated pods, 3× resources, 1× timeout, thinking off, medium effort for Sonnet, and Opus 4.6 as advisor. The SWE-bench headline combines an architectural change with thinking/prompt changes; it cannot isolate the advisor’s causal contribution. [21]

**Cognition’s 2026 account is more nuanced than its 2025 slogan.** The original article stresses full relevant context and the implicit decisions carried by write actions. The follow-up endorses a “one writer, additional intelligence” arrangement: clean-context review, an advisory “smart friend,” and managed decomposition. It says SWE-1.5 as a weak primary could not reliably decide when or what to escalate; cross-frontier Claude/GPT consultation worked in production, but it supplies no controlled effect size for that combination. These are first-party engineering observations, not peer-reviewed architecture comparisons. [10][11]

**The newest team experiments challenge any permanent single-writer law.** DeepSeek’s September 2026 report describes persistent asynchronous teammates sharing one checkout, a mailbox, task ownership/dependencies, and advisory write scopes, followed by lead review/testing. It trains team use with task reward, a collaboration bonus, and a critical-path-derived latency penalty. The reported gains concern this jointly trained model/harness configuration; they do not prove that bolting the same topology onto arbitrary models will help. [23]

**Contemporary negative coding evidence also exists.** Anthropic’s August 2026 research account finds independent vulnerability search and a coordinating swarm complementary, but corrects a tempting headline: its swarm searched more locations and spent more tokens. Restricting comparison to core directories made the approaches comparable in tokens per vulnerability. In its game-building experiments, successful PR merging and code sharing varied by model generation, yet end products remained poor. Coordination throughput is not the same as useful software. [22]

### 5. Limits, error compounding, and failure taxonomies

MAST’s 14 failure modes include specification/role violations, repetition, context loss, failure to recognize completion, conversation resets, unjustified assumptions, derailment, information withholding, ignored input, reasoning–action mismatch, premature termination, absent/incomplete verification, and incorrect verification. Its failure-distribution charts are expressly **not** rankings across systems with different tasks. [7]

The MAST coding example of a program that compiles but violates chess rules illustrates why a verifier role is insufficient by itself. Its intervention combines higher-level requirements with iterative verification; ProgramDev-v0 success rises from **25.0%** to **40.6%**, still far from reliable completion. The paper’s main text calls the change a “15.6%” improvement; the table makes clear that this is a **15.6-percentage-point** absolute change. [7]

The current *Science of Scaling* preprint reports gains on decomposable financial reasoning and losses on sequential planning, but its own limitations matter. It does not optimize prompts for each model/topology; coding and terminal evaluations use only 20 instances each, with typical bootstrap interval widths around **±20 percentage points**. The capability-saturation finding is more robust than several tool/coordination regression effects after clustered inference. Its approximate **45%** baseline threshold is a study-specific empirical heuristic, not a rule for disabling agents in a coding harness. [8]

**[INFERENCE] Error compounding is not simply “more agents means more errors.”** Independent candidate generation can let voting or tests reject errors, while dependent handoffs can transmit an incorrect premise into later actions. What matters is the dependency graph, correlated mistakes, evidence availability, and whether a verification bottleneck intercepts failures. The positive ensemble studies and the failure/scaling studies examine different points in that design space. [3][4][7][8]

### 6. Judging and review: evidence access before reviewer identity

- **LLM judges can be useful and biased at the same time.** Zheng et al. find substantial agreement with human preferences while documenting position, verbosity, self-enhancement, and reasoning limitations. Panickssery et al. provide targeted evidence of self-recognition and self-preference on summarization, with controlled fine-tuning experiments. Neither establishes a universal property of every model or every coding reviewer. [12][24]
- **Family diversity is a hedge, not a guarantee.** *Play Favorites* estimates quality-controlled self- and family-bias and finds it for models including GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet. It studies judged completions, not defect-detection recall in repository code. **[INFERENCE]** A different model family is a reasonable experimental reviewer choice; “always use a different family because it catches more code bugs” exceeds the evidence reviewed here. [12][25]
- **Code introduces its own biases.** Moon et al. show that superficial changes to semantically equivalent code affect scores across tested judges and languages, persisting even when judges are prompted to generate test cases before scoring. Generating tests in text is not the same as executing a complete verification suite. [18]
- **Agent-as-a-Judge tests a more relevant mechanism.** It lets an evaluator locate/read artifacts against requirements. On OpenHands, black-box alignment with human consensus reaches **90.44%**, compared with **60.38%** for its LLM-as-a-Judge baseline. The paper warns about class imbalance and works with relatively small generated projects; this is not a guarantee of production bug detection. It also says direct corrective use of the feedback is not evaluated in this proof of concept. [17]
- **Fresh-context review is not necessarily cross-model review.** Cognition reports its own coder/reviewer loop working best without prior context sharing, followed by reconciliation with the coder’s task context. **[INFERENCE]** An independent reviewer should inspect the diff and necessary requirements/source, without inheriting the author’s entire persuasive narrative; the integrator should adjudicate findings rather than blindly applying them. Exact context policy remains task-dependent. [11][17]

### 7. Evidence-based best-practice principles

Confidence below concerns the **conditional recommendation**, not a universal architecture ranking. **High** means converging evidence plus direct task relevance; **medium** means limited controlled support and/or converging first-party engineering accounts; **low** means plausible extrapolation with missing direct tests. All prescriptive synthesis is **[INFERENCE]**.

1. **Start with a strong simple baseline; require an observed reason to add coordination. — High.** Compare against a well-prompted single loop, a fixed pipeline where appropriate, and matched-budget repeated sampling. Weak prompts and unequal test-time compute can make extra agents look uniquely powerful. [2][3][5][8][20]
2. **Parallelize separable information gathering or independent candidate work first. — High.** Parallel scouts, source research, and bounded independent audits minimize conflicting writes. Do not split a tightly sequential reasoning chain merely to increase agent count. [8][9][19][22]
3. **For coupled edits, retain one integration owner; use multi-writer teams only with explicit ownership and dependency handling. — Medium.** Defaulting to one writer is a conservative engineering pattern, not a permanent theorem; current trained team systems demonstrate alternatives. File separation alone is insufficient if agents independently change shared interfaces or assumptions. [10][11][22][23]
4. **Define tasks as contracts, not job titles. — High.** Include the objective, permitted scope/tools, dependencies, expected artifact, evidence requirements, and completion criteria. MAST and Anthropic both document duplication, gaps, and misinterpretation when delegation is vague. [7][9][13]
5. **Choose models by measured role performance, not brand diversity. — High.** Preserve strong proposer/editor/aggregator quality; benchmark weaker workers on their actual role. X-MAS supports role-specific selection and Self-MoA warns against diluting quality merely to mix models. [1][2][6][19]
6. **Cheap scouts plus a strong planner are a useful option, not a fixed shopping list. — Medium.** HyperAgent gives direct early evidence; newer models and repositories need retesting. A weak executor plus advisor is another option, but escalation calibration can be the bottleneck. [19][21][11]
7. **Preserve consequential decisions and provenance in handoffs. — High.** Return exact artifact references, relevant source locations, performed actions, observed checks, remaining uncertainty, and assumptions that downstream work depends on. Keep bulky logs retrievable rather than repeatedly paraphrasing them through the coordinator. [7][9][10][19]
8. **Use an evidence-seeking reviewer and external checks; distinguish “reviewed” from “verified.” — High.** A reviewer should examine implementation and requirements; tests/validators should cover behavior and integration, not just syntax. Keep human adjudication for consequential ambiguity and taste. [7][17][18][22]
9. **Try cross-family review as an ablation, not an assurance. — Low for superior defect detection; medium as a bias-mitigation hypothesis.** Compare it with an equally capable same-model fresh-context reviewer using blinded defects, precision/recall, and cost. Existing self/family-bias evidence motivates the experiment but does not answer it. [12][18][25][11]
10. **Control budget, latency, and communication separately. — High.** Bound calls/rounds, set completion criteria, avoid repeated full-history copying, and report total tokens/cost alongside wall-clock and quality. Faster parallel completion can consume more total compute; the relevant optimum depends on which resource the user values. [3][9][15][16][21][23]
11. **Re-evaluate after changing model or harness. — High.** Model-role rankings and coordination behavior vary across studies and model generations. Record model IDs, prompt/tool configuration, benchmark split, repetitions, and date rather than promoting a historical result into an evergreen setting recommendation. [6][8][19][21][22]

## Contested or uncertain

- **No universal winning topology.** Static response synthesis, independent search, repository repair, and concurrent shared-state development are different tasks. Positive results in one do not resolve the others. Strong prompting, model choice, and budget are important confounders. [1][3][5][8][19][23]
- **The scaling paper materially changed.** v1 (2025-12-09) reports 180 configurations/four benchmarks, cross-validated **R² = 0.513**, and **+80.9%** financial improvement; v3 (2026-04-08) reports 260/six, **0.373** (or **0.413** with task-grounded capability), and **+80.8%**. Use v3 for current claims. v3 also adds statistical caveats and small coding/terminal samples. [8]
- **The current scaling manuscript has internal presentation inconsistencies.** §4.1 says eight models on each coding benchmark, while Table 16 displays nine rows including Gemini-3-Flash. Its robustness text also varies in how many clustered-inference terms retain significance. This dossier therefore treats its directional message as suggestive and does not recompute or promote the aggregate coding numbers into a precise universal law. [8]
- **The strongest-contributor question is unresolved.** Anthropic’s research result changes both team structure and model allocation and spends more tokens. HyperAgent changes role-model allocation. DeepSeek reports strongest observed configurations under deadlines. None alone isolates a timeless “collaboration bonus” over an equally optimized, equally resourced single agent. [9][19][23]
- **“Share all context” versus “fresh context” is not a simple contradiction.** Cognition emphasizes shared consequential decisions for write actions, but its later review experiments favor no initial context sharing. Anthropic favors selective findings plus durable artifacts. The relevant distinction is required task evidence versus incidental trajectory, and the best compression policy has not been established generally. [9][10][11]
- **The science does not invalidate product engineering reports, or vice versa.** The former often controls narrow tasks and budgets; the latter observes real products but can lack disclosed baselines, confidence intervals, and independent replication. Their evidentiary roles should remain visibly different. [7][8][9][11][21][22]

### Where the evidence is thin

1. **Cross-family code-review advantage:** no directly opened controlled study establishes greater real-defect recall/precision than a capability-matched, fresh-context same-model reviewer. Self/family-preference studies concern other output-evaluation settings; code surface-bias studies do not settle reviewer-family choice. [12][18][25]
2. **A universal cheap-scout/strong-planner prescription:** HyperAgent supplies a concrete early example and role ablations, but its model generations are old and its ablation subset is small. Current advisor results investigate a different topology and change prompts/thinking. [19][21]
3. **omp-specific causal performance:** none of these studies evaluates omp. Applying their principles to omp settings is an explicit extrapolation requiring separate product-source mapping and user-workload evaluation. [1]–[25]
4. **Long-term maintainability and human taste:** patch tests, task scores, PR counts, and judge agreement are not six-month maintenance outcomes. Anthropic’s game experiment explicitly demonstrates poor end products despite some successful coordination metrics. [17][19][22]
5. **Large concurrent editing teams at equal dollars/tokens:** DeepSeek’s current evidence is preliminary and deadline-based; Anthropic’s swarm comparisons have scope/token differences. Neither establishes a cost-optimal agent count for normal repository work. [22][23]
6. **Optimal handoff schema, stopping criterion, and number of review cycles:** engineering accounts provide useful mechanisms, but the opened studies do not establish a single cross-model optimum. Blindly specifying fixed agent counts or review rounds would be guessing. [7][9][11][19]
7. **Independent replication of vendor headline gains:** the 90.2% research improvement, advisor cost/quality gains, and latest team results are first-party results under their stated conditions. They should be narrated as reported findings, not guaranteed user outcomes. [9][21][23]

## Corrections & nuances to the blueprint

- Replace “what mixture-of-agents architecture is best practice” with **“which multi-agent pattern fits which task, evidence, and budget?”** Capitalized MoA is one specific ensemble architecture. [1][8]
- Do not equate **more agents**, **more model families**, **more tokens**, **parallel tool calls**, and **isolated contexts**. These are separable interventions. [1][2][3][8][9][15]
- MoA’s **65.1%** is a historical AlpacaEval LC win rate, not coding accuracy, a 2026 frontier claim, or an omp benchmark. [1]
- Self-MoA’s **6.6** is an absolute point difference; its main comparison retains a different aggregator model. It is more precise to say “same-model proposer sampling” than “one model does absolutely everything.” [2]
- Anthropic’s **15×** denominator is chat, not a single autonomous agent. Its **90.2%** is an internal research-evaluation comparison, not SWE-bench. [9]
- Cognition is not permanently “anti-multi-agent”: include the April 2026 follow-up and its preference for controlled writes plus added intelligence. [10][11]
- Strong planner/cheap workers is not the only supported option: the advisor pattern reverses control, and newer models are being trained specifically for team use. [21][23]
- Cross-family review is a **testable recommendation with low direct coding evidence**, not an established universal best practice. [12][18][25]
- Current papers do not show omp uniquely owns these architectural ideas. Product differentiation must come from current implementation and ergonomics, not claims that other harnesses cannot use subagents or multiple models. [9][11][21][23]

## Open questions for the user

- Should the recommended workflow optimize **lowest cost**, **lowest wall-clock latency**, **highest validated success**, or a specified balance? This determines whether parallel speculation and expensive review are acceptable.
- Which demonstration best represents the audience: a small bug fix, a multi-file feature, unfamiliar-repository research, security review, or a long-running migration?
- Are recommendations allowed to assume access to multiple paid model providers, or should there also be a single-provider/local-model configuration?
- Should narration use the precise term **MoA** only for Wang et al.’s architecture, or introduce an explicitly broader informal “mixture of agents” label?
- How much screen time should be devoted to limitations and counterevidence versus the practical omp walkthrough? The conclusions must remain qualified even if the methodological appendix is shortened.
